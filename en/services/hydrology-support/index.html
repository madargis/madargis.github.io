GIS Support for Hydrology Studies

Elevation model, catchments, wadi flow paths and report maps prepared for the study engineer at a qualified office.

The problem

A study demands a lot of geospatial work: an elevation model, catchments and wadi flow paths, land use and soil maps, a rain gauge map. A small office has nobody to do it quickly.

Who it is for

Consulting offices qualified for hydrology studies, and engineers and consultants who need mapping support for their studies. The service is for offices, not individuals.

A starting price. The final price is written in a one-page offer.

What you receive

  • An elevation model prepared for the study area.
  • Catchments and flow paths with their morphometric parameters in a table.
  • Land use and soil layers.
  • A rain gauge map and its data table as supplied by the office.
  • Report maps in the office's template, and flood maps from the engineer's model results if requested.
  • A one-page method note.

What you supply

  • Site boundary and corner coordinates.
  • The topographic survey, if any.
  • Rainfall data.
  • The required outlet points.
  • The map template.
  • Model results, if flood maps are requested.

How the work goes

  1. The office fills in the request form, with the project location, area, deadline and the responsible engineer's name.
  2. We agree with the engineer on outlet points, the elevation source and the coordinate reference.
  3. We send a one-page offer that separates what we deliver from what stays with the engineer.
  4. We prepare the elevation model: clipping, sink filling and merging the field survey if there is one.
  5. We derive flow direction and accumulation, then catchments and flow paths at the agreed outlets.
  6. We compare the flow paths with recent satellite imagery and the field survey, and document where they differ.
  7. We prepare the land use and soil layers and compute the morphometric parameters.
  8. We produce the maps in the office's template.
  9. We deliver the layers, the table, the maps and the method note to the engineer, with one round of comments. The acceptance period is 7 days.

Accuracy and limits statement

Geospatial support outputs for the study engineer, not a hydrology study. Catchments are derived from the Copernicus DEM at 30 m, with a stated vertical accuracy of under 4 m. It is a surface model that includes buildings, from imagery acquired between 2011 and 2015. It suits catchment delineation in clear terrain and does not suit flood depths in cities and plains. A field survey of the site is required.

Regulated work

A hydrology study is prepared and signed by a qualified office. Rainfall frequency analysis, model decisions, design and signing stay with the engineer.

Out of scope

  • Rainfall frequency analysis and design storms
  • Hydrological and hydraulic modelling
  • Channel and culvert design
  • Signing
  • Field surveying
  • Requesting rainfall data
