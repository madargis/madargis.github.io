Desk screening of building exposure to wadi channels in Muhayil Asir

We classed 2,902 OpenStreetMap buildings in Muhayil Asir by their height above and distance from the nearest wadi channel, using NASADEM. 71 buildings fall in the high class and 295 in the medium class.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

An owner, developer or agency wants to know, before any study, which buildings or plots lie in or near a wadi channel at a low level, to decide where a hydrology study is needed.

The data

NASADEM_HGT version 001 by NASA JPL (2020): 1 arc-second (about 30 m) elevations from the SRTM flight of February 2000, in whole metres above the EGM96 geoid, tiles n18e041 and n18e042. Only a window was read from Microsoft Planetary Computer (collection nasadem, no account) on 20 November 2026. Licence: openly shared without restriction under the NASA EOSDIS guidance. Citation: NASA JPL. (2020). NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc second V001 [Data set]. NASA Land Processes Distributed Active Archive Center. https://doi.org/10.5067/MEASURES/NASADEM/NASADEM_HGT.001. Buildings: every closed way tagged building in OpenStreetMap inside a 7 × 7 km box, through the Overpass API on the same day, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL.

The method

  1. Read an 85 × 75 km window (EPSG:32638, x 160000 to 245000, y 2015000 to 2090000) covering the mountains that drain to the town, and reprojected it to 30 m cells.
  2. Filled depressions by priority flood, derived D8 flow direction and accumulation, and checked that the catchments draining to the town's channels do not touch the window edge.
  3. Wadi channel: every cell draining 5 km² or more. For each building, at a point inside it: height above the nearest channel along the flow path (HAND) and horizontal distance to the nearest channel.
  4. Classes: high = 2 m or less above the channel and within 100 m of it; medium = 5 m or less and within 300 m; low = the rest.
  5. Reclassified with channel thresholds of 2 and 10 km², and with both height limits doubled, to measure the effect of these choices.

The result

Of 2,902 buildings, 71 are high (2.4%), 295 medium (10.2%) and 2,536 low (87.4%). The main wadi through the town drains 631 km², and the town's channels together drain 647 km². 27 buildings stand on cells draining 1 km² or more. The median building sits 15 m above and 551 m from the nearest channel. With a 2 km² channel threshold the high class grows to 96 and medium to 354; at 10 km² high stays 71; with both height limits doubled high becomes 125 and medium 524.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. This is a desk screening, not a flood study or a hydrology study: there is no rainfall, discharge, depth or return period, it is not submitted to any authority for plan or building approval, and the study must be prepared and signed by a qualified office. The NASADEM user guide (NASA JPL, 2020) gives the SRTM requirement as 16 m absolute vertical error at 90% and a global average absolute height error of about 6.8 m, larger than the 2 m and 5 m limits, so the classes rank buildings relative to each other and do not measure their levels. The model is a radar surface of February 2000: it misses later buildings, roads, culverts and channel works, and it lifts older buildings off the ground. A low class does not mean a building is safe: runoff in streets and gullies draining less than 5 km² is not covered. No past flood imagery was checked, and the completeness of OpenStreetMap buildings was not verified.
