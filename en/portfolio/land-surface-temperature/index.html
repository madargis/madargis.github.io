Land surface temperature in Riyadh, summer 2026, by land cover and district

One Landsat 9 scene on 24 July 2026 at about 10:20 am gives a median surface temperature of 52.4 °C over 130 Riyadh districts. Bare land is hotter (56.5 °C) than urban land use (52.2 °C), and green park pixels are cooler (47.7 °C).

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

A planner or greening consultant needs to know which districts and surfaces are hottest by day, to target shade and planting, from open data with a stated method.

The data

Landsat 9 Collection 2 Level-2, scene LC09_L2SP_166043_20260724_02_T1 (path 166 row 43, 24 July 2026, cloud 0%): surface temperature ST_B10, its uncertainty ST_QA, red and near-infrared surface reflectance and the QA_PIXEL layer, read as windows through the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC API (collection landsat-c2-l2) with anonymous signing and no account. Public domain at USGS; credit as USGS asks: Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 image courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey (doi.org/10.5066/P9OGBGM6). District boundaries (admin_level=10), parks, major roads, land use and bare-land areas from OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API, licence ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors. All downloaded on 20 November 2026.

The method

  1. Picked a cloud-free summer scene on path 166, which covers west and central Riyadh; path 165 cuts off the west.
  2. Converted ST_B10 to temperature: value × 0.00341802 + 149.0 kelvin, minus 273.15, and masked pixels that QA_PIXEL flags as cloud, shadow or fill.
  3. Districts: 130 of the 137 OpenStreetMap districts, after leaving out five eastern ones that fall partly outside the scene and two smaller than 18 ha (200 pixels). Each district's mean comes from the 30 m pixels whose centres fall inside it.
  4. Classes from OpenStreetMap: parks (leisure=park, 30 m in from the edge), green park pixels among them (NDVI ≥ 0.3 from the same scene), major roads (15 m either side of motorway, trunk and primary centrelines), residential, commercial and retail land use at least 60 m from those roads, and bare land (natural=sand, bare_rock, scree or desert).
  5. For each class: median, 25th and 75th percentile and pixel count.

The result

Median surface temperature over the 130 districts: 52.4 °C (half the pixels between 51.3 and 53.6 °C). Median by class: all parks 52.3 °C with a wide spread from 48.4 to 58.8 °C because most are not planted (median NDVI 0.10), green park pixels 47.7 °C, major roads 53.7 °C, residential and commercial land use 52.2 °C, bare land 56.5 °C. Hottest districts: As Sinaiyah 55.7 °C, Al Rimal 55.4, Al Nour 55.2, Ash Sharq 55.0, Al Rimayah 54.7. Coolest: Salam 47.6 °C, King Salman Park 48.8, Al Waha 50.0, Al Khuzama 50.4, Al Malga 50.5. Median surface-temperature uncertainty from ST_QA is ±1.59 K.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. Surface temperature is not air temperature, and one overpass at about 10:20 am represents neither the whole day nor the whole summer. The thermal band is 100 m resampled to 30 m, so a road pixel mixes the road with its surroundings, and small parks and districts (Salam is 35 ha) take on their neighbours' values. Classes come from OpenStreetMap, whose completeness is not guaranteed: a mapped park may be vacant land and residential land use includes unbuilt plots. Five eastern districts (among them Al Janadriyah and Al-Nazim) fall partly outside the scene and are not ranked. Uncertainty is about ±1.6 K, so differences under 2 °C between districts are not meaningful.
