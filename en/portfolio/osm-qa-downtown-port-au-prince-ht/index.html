Quality audit of OpenStreetMap buildings in Downtown Port-au-Prince, Haiti

Madar Quality Checker read 1,287 OpenStreetMap buildings in Downtown Port-au-Prince and found 9 with a geometry issue; 98.2% carry no street name.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

Spatial Data QA/QC and Accuracy Checks

The problem

A team that wants to reuse open building footprints in Downtown Port-au-Prince for a base map, an address project or a planning model needs to know how clean the geometry is and how complete the attributes are before it relies on them.

The data

OpenStreetMap building ways and multipolygon relations in the box -72.3450 to -72.3280 E and 18.5350 to 18.5500 N (298 ha), read through the Overpass API on 21 November 2026 (database state 2026-11-20), © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL (openstreetmap.org/copyright).

The method

  1. Downloaded every building way and multipolygon relation in the box and kept those whose inside point falls in it.
  2. Built polygons from closed ways and from the outer and inner rings of relations; 0 unclosed building ways could not form a polygon and were set aside.
  3. Ran Madar Quality Checker on the layer in EPSG:4326 with osm_id as the unique identifier, addr:street as a required attribute and overlaps under 1 m² ignored.
  4. Counted invalid, duplicate and overlapping geometry, empty identifiers and missing street names, and measured how many buildings carry a specific type and a number of levels.

The result

1,287 buildings with 30.5 ha of footprint. Geometry: 0 invalid geometry, 0 duplicate geometry, 5 overlap over 1 m², touching 9 buildings. Identifiers: 0 duplicates. Attributes: 1,264 buildings (98.2%) have no addr:street, 93.0% are typed only building=yes and 1.7% record a number of levels.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. The audit checks the consistency of the open layer, not its truth on the ground: a footprint can be valid and still misplaced, outdated or missing. OpenStreetMap is edited by volunteers and changes daily, so these counts describe the stated database state only. Many buildings in a real place have no street address at all, so a missing addr:street is a gap for an address project, not always an error. Overlaps under 1 m² were ignored. This is not an official survey deliverable.
