Lake Naivasha, Kenya: open-water area each year from Sentinel-2, 2017 to 2026

Lake Naivasha held 16,454 ha of open water on 27 January 2026, 2,143 ha more than the 14,312 ha of 8 February 2017. Over 10 usable years the area ranged from 14,214 to 16,454 ha.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

The Rift Valley lakes of Kenya, Lake Naivasha among them, have risen since about 2010 and flooded farms, homes and roads along their shores. A water authority or consultant wants a consistent yearly record of how much open water Lake Naivasha holds at the same time of year, from open imagery, to set beside gauge and storage records.

The data

Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance (Copernicus programme), bands B03, B11 and the scene classification layer, plus B02 and B04 for the true-colour panels, read as windows of cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs through the Earth Search STAC API (Element 84, collection sentinel-2-l2a) on 20 November 2026. Scenes: S2A_36MZE_20170208_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20180208_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20190203_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20200208_0_L2A, S2A_36MZE_20210227_1_L2A, S2A_36MZE_20220113_0_L2A, S2A_36MZE_20230128_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20240217_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20250221_0_L2A, S2B_36MZE_20260127_0_L2A. Attribution as the Copernicus Sentinel legal notice requires: Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2017-2026. Locator: Natural Earth, public domain.

The method

  1. For each year from 2017 to 2026, searched January and February scenes with under 40% tile cloud and kept the one with the most clear pixels in a 29 × 27 km window (10 of 10 years had one).
  2. Resampled to 20 m in EPSG:32737, masked no-data, cloud, cirrus and cloud shadow with the scene classification layer.
  3. Water: MNDWI = (B03 − B11) / (B03 + B11) above 0; median areas recounted with thresholds -0.1 and +0.1.
  4. Zone: every pixel that was water in at least one year and connected to the water body at 36.3500, -0.7700, widened by 3 pixels (18,113 ha).
  5. A year counts only when at least 97% of the zone is clear; 0 years were dropped for cloud over the water.

The result

Open water by year: 2017: 14,312 ha; 2018: 14,214 ha; 2019: 14,662 ha; 2020: 14,678 ha; 2021: 15,868 ha; 2022: 16,363 ha; 2023: 16,215 ha; 2024: 15,927 ha; 2025: 15,947 ha; 2026: 16,454 ha. First 14,312 ha (8 February 2017), latest 16,454 ha (27 January 2026), a change of +2,143 ha (+15%). Largest 16,454 ha in 2026, smallest 14,214 ha in 2018, median 15,898 ha (15,977 to 15,808 ha at MNDWI -0.1 and +0.1).

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. One scene per year, so each value is a snapshot of that day, not a yearly mean or the true minimum. Area is not volume or storage: the same area can hold very different volumes. At 20 m, edges, narrow arms and floating vegetation are uncertain by about one pixel along the shore; sediment-laden, very shallow or plant-covered water can be missed and wet mud or dark shadow counted. Cloud masks are imperfect. Gauge levels and storage records from the operator remain the reference.
