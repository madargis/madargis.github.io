Ranking candidate sites for a new cafe in Al Malqa, Riyadh

We scored 176 sites on the district's main roads against four criteria with stated weights. The top site scores 0.71 of 1, with about 9,021 people (WorldPop estimate) within a 10-minute walk.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

A cafe owner choosing among many sites in one district needs a documented comparison, not impressions, before signing a lease.

The data

OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API on 2026-11-20: the Al Malqa boundary, walking network, cafes and complementary places. Licence ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors. Population: WorldPop 2026 estimates at 100 m (R2025A, DOI 10.5258/SOTON/WP00839), licence CC BY 4.0, downloaded 2026-11-20.

The method

  1. Candidates: nodes on primary, secondary and tertiary roads inside the district, one per 300 m grid square, 176 in all.
  2. OpenStreetMap walking network at 4.8 km/h: 10 min = 800 m and 5 min = 400 m along the network.
  3. Population: sum of WorldPop cells reached within a 10-minute walk, each cell joined to its nearest network node.
  4. Competitors: cafes (amenity=cafe) within 10 min. Complements: shops, offices, restaurants, banks, schools, clinics and gyms within 5 min.
  5. Road: primary 1, secondary 0.67, tertiary 0.33.
  6. Each criterion is rescaled 0 to 1 across candidates, competitors inverted. Weights: population 0.40, complements 0.25, fewer competitors 0.20, road class 0.15.
  7. Sensitivity check: rerank with equal weights of 0.25.

The result

Top 5: #1: 0.71 (people 9,021, cafes 2, complements 2); #2: 0.61 (people 6,703, cafes 2, complements 2); #3: 0.60 (people 776, cafes 1, complements 5); #4: 0.50 (people 5,742, cafes 1, complements 0); #5: 0.43 (people 744, cafes 0, complements 1). 4 of 5 stay in the top five under equal weights. The district holds an estimated 19,262 people and 9 cafes mapped in OpenStreetMap, and 77% of candidates have no mapped complement within 5 minutes, which says more about mapping gaps than about the street.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. A spatial comparison: it does not forecast sales and is not a feasibility study. OpenStreetMap may miss recent cafes or businesses, and WorldPop is a modelled estimate of resident population at 100 m, not a count, with no footfall or spending power. Rent, vacancy, parking and street visibility are not scored. Candidates are network points, not available shops. The weights are an assumption for the demo; in a real project the client sets them.
