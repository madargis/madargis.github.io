Terrain pre-processing for the upper Wadi Hanifa catchment at Al-Uyaynah, north-west of Riyadh

From the 30 m NASADEM elevation model we derived flow direction, flow accumulation, the stream network and the catchment above an outlet near Al-Uyaynah. The catchment covers 414 km² and its longest flow path is 63.6 km.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

A qualified office preparing a hydrology study needs a conditioned DEM, the catchment boundary, its streams and its shape parameters quickly, with a stated source and accuracy, before its engineer starts the analysis and modelling.

The data

NASADEM_HGT version 001 by NASA JPL (2020): 1 arc-second (about 30 m) elevations from the SRTM flight of 11 to 21 February 2000, reprocessed by NASA, in whole metres above the EGM96 geoid, tiles n24e045, n24e046, n25e045 and n25e046. Only a window was read from the cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs on Microsoft Planetary Computer (collection nasadem, no account) on 20 November 2026. Licence: openly shared without restriction under the NASA EOSDIS Data Use and Citation Guidance. Citation: NASA JPL. (2020). NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc second V001 [Data set]. NASA Land Processes Distributed Active Archive Center. https://doi.org/10.5067/MEASURES/NASADEM/NASADEM_HGT.001. For comparison only: OpenStreetMap waterways through the Overpass API on 20 November 2026, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL.

The method

  1. Read a 46 × 40 km window (EPSG:32638, x 598000 to 644000, y 2731000 to 2771000) and reprojected it to 30 m cells by bilinear resampling.
  2. Conditioned the DEM: filled single-cell pits, then filled depressions, then resolved flats. 416 cells in flats were left without a flow direction, so they were routed along the priority-flood path to the spill point.
  3. Derived D8 flow direction and flow accumulation.
  4. Chose an outlet on Wadi Hanifa at 46.4033° E, 24.9014° N, snapped it to the nearest cell draining at least 100 km², delineated the catchment above it and checked that it does not touch the window edge.
  5. Streams: every cell draining 2 km² or more, recounted at 1 and 5 km² to measure the effect of the threshold.
  6. Computed area, longest flow path (D8 distance along the path), relief (highest point minus outlet) and mean slope from the unfilled DEM.
  7. Compared the streams with OpenStreetMap waterways inside the catchment using a 90 m distance in both directions.

The result

Catchment area 414 km². Longest flow path 63.6 km. Elevation runs from 718 m at the outlet to 1,021 m, a relief of 303 m. Mean slope 5.8° (10.3%). Streams at the 2 km² threshold total 290 km in 119 segments, a drainage density of 0.70 km/km²; the length is 390 km at 1 km² and 194 km at 5 km². Conditioning raised 11.5% of the catchment cells, by up to 15.0 m, mostly small pits scattered over plains and wadi floors. 70% of the derived stream length lies within 90 m of an OpenStreetMap waterway, while only 37% of the 541 km of OpenStreetMap waterways lies near a derived stream, because OpenStreetMap includes gullies smaller than the threshold.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. This is terrain pre-processing that supports a hydrology study prepared and signed by a qualified office. It is not a hydrology study or a flood study: there is no rainfall, discharge or water depth. The NASADEM user guide (NASA JPL, 2020) gives the SRTM requirement as 16 m absolute vertical error at 90% and a global average absolute height error of about 6.8 m; heights are stored in whole metres. It is a radar surface model of February 2000: later dams, roads and buildings are missing, and NASA filled radar voids mainly from ASTER GDEM. On this flat plateau the result depends on how flats are routed: without routing the 416 cells the flow stops at a flat near the Al-Haisiyah dam and the catchment is 261 km². The Al-Haisiyah dam mapped in OpenStreetMap lies inside the catchment, and dams, bridges and culverts can change flow paths or the divide. D8 paths follow 30 m cells, and slope computed from them is lower than the true slope on steep faces. The boundary and outlet must be checked against a field survey and recent imagery before any use.
