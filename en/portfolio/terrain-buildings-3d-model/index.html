Terrain and buildings block model in 3D, central Abha

We built a 3D model of a 2 × 2 km window in central Abha from NASA's NASADEM and OpenStreetMap buildings, exported as one 1.7 MB GLB file. It holds 80,000 terrain triangles and 284 buildings extruded from the ground.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

Demo date2026-11-20

Related service3D Mesh Models

The problem

A design office, developer or researcher needs a light 3D model of an area with its terrain and buildings at their heights, to show in a browser or open in design software, with a stated source and stated limits.

The data

NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc second V001 by NASA JPL (1 arc-second grid, about 30 m, reprocessed from the February 2000 SRTM radar data, heights relative to the EGM96 geoid), tile NASADEM_HGT_n18e042; a window was read without an account from the Microsoft Planetary Computer (STAC collection nasadem, anonymous SAS token) on 2026-11-20. Licence: NASA's product page says the dataset “is openly shared, without restriction”, and NASA's EOSDIS data use guidance licenses data from NASA-led missions as CC0 (a public domain dedication). Citation as NASA asks: NASA JPL. (2020). NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc second V001 [Data set]. NASA Land Processes Distributed Active Archive Center. https://doi.org/10.5067/MEASURES/NASADEM/NASADEM_HGT.001. Buildings: OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API on 2026-11-20, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL.

The method

  1. Chose the window: 2 × 2 km around 42.505° E, 18.2165° N in central Abha, because its relief is 94 m, above the 60 m required, and OpenStreetMap maps 284 buildings in it.
  2. Reprojected the DEM to UTM 38N (EPSG:32638) and resampled it to a 10 m grid by bilinear interpolation. Resampling adds no detail to a 30 m model; it only smooths the surface between its posts.
  3. Turned the grid into a triangle mesh: 40,401 vertices and 80,000 triangles, not simplified because the file stays under 8 MB without it.
  4. Extruded each building from the lowest terrain height under its footprint to its roof: from the height tag, else building:levels × 3 m, else a default 6 m.
  5. Recentred the model near the origin with Y up as glTF expects, and set vertex colours: terrain by elevation, buildings by height source.
  6. Exported terrain and buildings as two separate meshes in one GLB file, then loaded it back to check the mesh count and size.

The result

Window 2.0 × 2.0 km (4 km²). Elevation runs from 2,189 to 2,283 m, a relief of 94 m. Grid spacing 10 m. Terrain 80,000 triangles, buildings 4,884 triangles, 84,884 in total. 284 buildings: 0 from the height tag, 0 from building:levels and 284 at the default height. GLB file size 1.71 MB. No building in the window carries a height or building:levels tag, so every height is assumed, and the count reflects what OpenStreetMap maps, not the buildings on the ground.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. It is a first sample of Madar's own open-source pipeline. The terrain is NASADEM, a radar surface model of about 30 m captured in February 2000. NASA's user guide gives the SRTM specification as 16 m absolute vertical error at the 90% level and cites a global assessment of about 6.8 m average absolute height error; NASADEM reprocesses the same radar data. It includes part of the buildings and trees standing in 2000 and knows nothing built or graded since, so it is not survey-grade bare-earth terrain. The buildings are simple extrusions (level of detail 1) with no roof shapes or facades, and 284 of them carry an assumed height because the tags are missing; this is not a photogrammetric or LiDAR mesh. OpenStreetMap footprints are drawn by volunteers and can be incomplete or out of date. Not fit for engineering design or quantities without a field survey.
