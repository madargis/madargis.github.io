Urban expansion in Al Narjis district, Riyadh: October 2017 to October 2026

Two Sentinel-2 scenes nine years apart show 1,128 ha of new urban fabric inside Al Narjis district (4,816 ha). In a sample of 100 detections where OpenStreetMap buildings are mapped, 50 had a building within 15 m.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

An organisation watching a fast-growing district wants to know where new built-up land appeared between two dates, how much, and which sites to check on the ground, without commercial imagery.

The data

Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance, 10 m, tile 38RPN: scene S2A_38RPN_20171023_0_L2A (23 October 2017, processing baseline 00.01, tile cloud 0.07%) and scene S2C_38RPN_20261016_0_L2A (16 October 2026, baseline 05.13, tile cloud 0.007%), read as windows of cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs through the Earth Search STAC API (Element 84, collection sentinel-2-l2a). Attribution as the Copernicus Sentinel legal notice requires: Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2017, 2026. District boundary (relation 13254378), 20,357 current buildings in the window and the 106 buildings mapped there as of 23 October 2017, from OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API, licence ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors. All downloaded on 20 November 2026.

The method

  1. Picked two clear scenes in the same month (October) nine years apart and read only an 11.5 × 18.7 km window (EPSG:32638) of the blue, green and red bands and the SCL layer.
  2. SCL cloud masks were not used: the 2017 scene flags 5.9% of the district as cloud, all of it bright roofs and an airport runway in a clear scene, so a false alarm.
  3. Urban-fabric index: local standard deviation of mean visible brightness in a 5 × 5 pixel (50 m) window, divided by its median over a 1 km² patch of undeveloped sand east of the district, which removes the calibration difference between the dates.
  4. A pixel is built-up when the ratio is 4 or more. New = built-up in 2026 only, after dropping patches smaller than 4 pixels (400 m²).
  5. Repeated the count at ratios 3 and 5 to measure sensitivity.
  6. Compared a random sample of 100 new pixels and 100 pixels undeveloped on both dates with OpenStreetMap buildings, only inside 500 m blocks where mapped buildings cover at least 10%, because buildings are not mapped in the north of the district.

The result

District area 4,816 ha. Urban fabric covered 2,287 ha in October 2017 and 3,115 ha in October 2026; new urban fabric is 1,128 ha (928 to 1,274 ha at ratios 3 and 5). 302 ha were flagged built-up in 2017 but not in 2026, which is not physically plausible and measures the noise of the method. OpenStreetMap check (mapped zone of 786 ha holding 160 ha of detections): 50 of 100 detections had a building within 15 m, none of them mapped by 2017, and 50 had none (roads, yards, works or false alarms). In the undeveloped control sample: 11 of 100.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. At 10 m, anything smaller than about 400 m² is not reliably detected. The index measures urban fabric (buildings, roads, paving and construction), not buildings alone, so the figures are neither a building count nor a roof area. Noise is about ±300 ha. The OpenStreetMap check is a proxy, not field verification: the map holds no construction dates, held only 106 buildings in and around the district in 2017, and covers a small part of the district today. The output is a list of candidate sites to verify, not proof of a violation or a legal finding.
