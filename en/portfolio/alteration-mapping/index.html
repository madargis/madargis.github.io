Desk screening of hydrothermal-alteration indicators around Mahd adh Dhahab from Landsat 9

We computed iron-oxide and clay band ratios from a clear Landsat 9 scene over a 30 × 30 km window around the Mahd adh Dhahab mine. High values of both ratios overlap on 0.7 km², and 19% of that lies within 2 km of the mine's two records.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

An exploration team or licence-round bidder wants a fast screening of an Arabian Shield area before field work, to decide where to start sampling, without buying commercial imagery.

The data

Landsat 9 OLI-2 Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance, scene LC09_L2SP_169044_20261001_02_T1 (1 October 2026, path 169 row 44, scene cloud 0.0%, sun elevation 56.7°), bands B2, B3, B4, B5, B6, B7 and QA_PIXEL at 30 m. A 30 × 30 km window was read from Microsoft Planetary Computer (collection landsat-c2-l2, no account) on 20 November 2026. Licence: USGS Landsat data carry no restrictions. Credit: Landsat 9 image courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. Data DOI: https://doi.org/10.5066/P9OGBGM6. Deposit locations: two records named Mahd Adh Dhahab (Au, Ag, Cu) from the USGS Mineral Resources Data System through its WFS service on the same day; citation: U.S. Geological Survey, 2005, Mineral Resources Data System: U.S. Geological Survey, Reston, Virginia.

The method

  1. Searched Landsat 8 and 9 scenes with less than 1% cloud over the window from 2024 to November 2026, and picked the clear Landsat 9 scene of 1 October 2026 (sun elevation 57°).
  2. Read a 30 × 30 km window (EPSG:32637, x 675015 to 705015, y 2585015 to 2615015), 1,000 × 1,000 pixels, and converted it to reflectance: value × 0.0000275 − 0.2.
  3. Masked cloud, cloud shadow and water from QA_PIXEL, vegetation (NDVI above 0.25) and dark pixels (reflectance below 0.01): 0.07% of the window.
  4. Ratios: iron oxide B4/B2, clay, hydroxyl minerals and carbonate B6/B7, ferrous iron B6/B5.
  5. Anomaly: ratio above the window mean + 2 standard deviations, recounted at 1.5 and 2.5.
  6. Compared the overlap with the two MRDS records: its share within 2 km of them divided by the window's share within 2 km.

The result

In the 900 km² window, high iron-oxide values cover 10.5 km² and high clay values 18.5 km², overlapping on 0.7 km² (0.08%). 19% of the overlap lies within 2 km of the two Mahd adh Dhahab records in MRDS, while that zone is 2.8% of the window, a concentration 7.0 times the window average. The largest clay patch is beside the northern record and the town; the largest iron-oxide-only cluster is south-east of the town.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. This is a desk screening that suggests where field work could start. It is not a mineral discovery, a prospect evaluation or a resource estimate, and a geologist must check it in the field. Pixels are 30 m and Landsat has only two short-wave infrared bands, so the clay ratio groups clays, micas and carbonates and cannot name a mineral. The same ratios rise over mine waste, buildings and roads: the clay patch beside the northern record, a producing mine, and the speckle in the town may be of this kind, so the concentration near the mine may reflect mining, not alteration alone. Iron coatings on sand and gravel and desert varnish give an iron-oxide signal. The mean + 2 SD threshold is a convention: the overlap is 0.15 km² at 2.5 SD and 3.5 km² at 1.5 SD. One scene was used with no geological map and no samples, so no accuracy figure can be given. The two MRDS records share one name and lie 3.8 km apart, so the position is approximate.
