Walking catchment of Mulago National Referral Hospital, Uganda

On the OpenStreetMap path network, 134 ha and 2,240 mapped buildings lie within a 15-minute walk of Mulago National Referral Hospital, 30% of the straight-line circle of the same radius.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

A health or planning team in Kampala wants to know which streets and how many buildings can reach Mulago National Referral Hospital on foot within 5, 10 and 15 minutes, and where the street network makes the walk longer than the map distance suggests.

The data

OpenStreetMap streets and paths (highway ways, 745.3 km in the connected network), buildings (100,533 in the window) and the facility way/45353329 (Mulago National Referal Hospital), read through the Overpass API on 20 November 2026 (database state 2026-11-20), © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL (openstreetmap.org/copyright).

The method

  1. Built a walking graph from every highway way except motorways, construction and private ways, and kept the largest connected part (38,220 nodes).
  2. Joined the facility to its nearest network node (17 m away).
  3. Walking speed 4.8 km/h, so 5, 10 and 15 minutes are 400, 800 and 1,200 m along the network; shortest paths by Dijkstra, cutting edges where the time runs out.
  4. Turned the reached streets into areas with a 50 m buffer and counted the OpenStreetMap buildings whose inside point falls in each.
  5. Compared each area with the straight-line circle of the same distance.

The result

Within 5 minutes: 8 ha, 1.2 km of street and 55 buildings. Within 10 minutes: 42 ha, 8.1 km and 416 buildings. Within 15 minutes: 134 ha, 27.4 km and 2,240 buildings. The network covers 16%, 21% and 30% of the straight-line circles of 50, 201 and 452 ha.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. Network analysis, not a transport or traffic study. Walking is modelled at a constant 4.8 km/h with no slopes, crossings, waiting, steps penalty, sidewalk quality, safety or opening hours. The result is only as complete as the OpenStreetMap paths: a missing footpath shrinks the catchment and a wrongly connected one enlarges it. Buildings are counted, not people, and one building can hold one household or hundreds. The 50 m buffer turns street lines into areas and is a convention, not a measured distance.
