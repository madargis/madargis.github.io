Overhead clearance screening along Avenue Berthelot, Lyon, from open LiDAR

Along 1.9 km of Avenue Berthelot, classified IGN LiDAR HD LiDAR shows 87 places where something hangs lower than 5 m over the road (4.54 per 100 m); the lowest counted overhead return is 2.5 m above the road.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

A haulier planning to move a load about 5 m high along Avenue Berthelot wants a first screening of where bridges, wires and tree branches may be too low, before a site survey and the permit application.

The data

IGN LiDAR HD classified point cloud (mission 21LHD6OL, flown 2021), tiles LHD_FXX_0844_6519_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, LHD_FXX_0842_6519_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, LHD_FXX_0842_6518_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, LHD_FXX_0843_6519_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, LHD_FXX_0844_6518_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, LHD_FXX_0843_6518_PTS_LAMB93_IGN69.copc.laz, read as COPC files from the IGN Géoplateforme (data.geopf.fr, anonymous access) on 20 November 2026; licence Licence Ouverte Etalab 2.0, credit © IGN, LiDAR HD. Road centreline: OpenStreetMap ways 2133545, 4231275, 4231276, 10409542, 16727540, 22823305 and others named Avenue Berthelot, through the Overpass API on 20 November 2026, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL (openstreetmap.org/copyright).

The method

  1. Took the OpenStreetMap centreline of Avenue Berthelot between the Rhône end of the avenue and Avenue Jean Jaurès and beyond (1,915 m) and moved it into EPSG:2154, the CRS of the LiDAR.
  2. Read only the points within 25 m of the route from the COPC files, in 100 m pieces (3,747,737 points).
  3. Stations every 1 m; road surface = median height of ground and road returns within 3 m of the centreline over 5 m, interpolated where the road is hidden (99% of stations measured directly).
  4. Overhead returns: every non-ground, non-noise point within 5 m of the centreline and 2.5 to 30 m above the road; the clearance at a station is the lowest of them, with its class as the data provider classified it. Only vegetation, bridge deck, codes 13 to 15, building and other structure classes count; unclassified returns, which include vehicles, are reported separately.
  5. Obstacle = a run of stations with clearance under the 5 m envelope, backed by at least 3 returns within 3 m, gaps up to 3 m joined; recounted at 4.5 and 5.5 m. Single stray returns are not counted.

The result

Route 1,915 m; 38.0 points per m² in the corridor. Something overhead lies within 30 m of the road over 900 m of the route. Lowest overhead return 2.5 m above the road, 602 m from the Rhône end of the avenue (vegetation). Inside the 5 m envelope: 87 obstacles (86 vegetation, 1 building) covering 624 m, 4.54 per 100 m; 83 at 4.5 m and 85 at 5.5 m. A further 6,162 unclassified returns lie between 2.5 and 5 m and were not counted.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. Screening, not a clearance survey or a route permit. Airborne LiDAR sees surfaces from above: for a bridge it records the top of the deck, so the true clearance under the soffit is lower by the deck depth, and thin wires and branches under a canopy can be missed. The data were flown in 2021; trees grow and are cut, and roads and bridges are rebuilt since. Returns below 2.5 m are not assessed, because vehicles parked or moving at the time of the flight look the same. Classes are the data provider's; codes 13 to 15 mean wire and tower in ASPRS LAS 1.4, but some projects use them for bridges or other structures, so they are shown by code. Heights are relative to the interpolated road surface and carry roughly the vertical accuracy of the LiDAR (about 0.1 m on open ground) plus the interpolation. Measure on site before any move.
