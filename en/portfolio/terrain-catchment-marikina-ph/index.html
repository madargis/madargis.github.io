Catchment and stream network of the Marikina River above Santo Niño, Marikina, Philippines

From the 30 m NASADEM terrain model we derived the catchment of the Marikina River above Santo Niño, Marikina: 517 km², 1,384 m of relief and a longest flow path of 68.5 km. It is terrain pre-processing for a hydrology study, not a hydrology study.

A demo built from open data, not a client project.

The problem

An engineering office preparing a drainage or hydrology study of the Marikina River near Santo Niño, Marikina needs the catchment boundary, a stream network and the basic shape parameters from a stated open source before its engineer starts the analysis.

The data

NASADEM_HGT version 001 by NASA JPL (2020): 1 arc-second (about 30 m) elevations from the SRTM flight of 11 to 22 February 2000, reprocessed by NASA, in metres above the EGM96 geoid, tiles n14e121. Only a 38 × 41 km window was read from the cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs on Microsoft Planetary Computer (collection nasadem, anonymous access) on 20 November 2026. Licence: openly shared without restriction under the NASA EOSDIS Data Use and Citation Guidance. Citation: NASA JPL (2020). NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc second V001. NASA Land Processes Distributed Active Archive Center. https://doi.org/10.5067/MEASURES/NASADEM/NASADEM_HGT.001. For comparison only: OpenStreetMap waterways through the Overpass API on 20 November 2026, © OpenStreetMap contributors, licence ODbL (openstreetmap.org/copyright).

The method

  1. Read a 38 × 41 km window and reprojected it to 30 m cells in EPSG:32651 by bilinear resampling.
  2. Conditioned the terrain with priority-flood depression filling and derived D8 flow directions and upstream area (pyflwdir), with flow leaving at the window edge.
  3. Placed the outlet on the Marikina River at 121.0945, 14.6340 and moved it downstream to the first cell draining at least 20 km² (127 m), then delineated the catchment above it and checked that it does not touch the window edge.
  4. Streams: every cell draining 1 km² or more, ordered by Strahler; drainage density recounted at 0.5 and 5 km² to show the effect of the threshold.
  5. Computed area, relief (highest point minus outlet), mean slope from the unconditioned terrain, longest flow path along D8 steps and the hypsometric integral.
  6. Compared the derived streams with OpenStreetMap waterways inside the catchment using a 90 m distance in both directions.

The result

Catchment area 517 km² above an outlet at 9 m; highest point 1,393 m, so relief 1,384 m. Mean slope 15.1°, longest flow path 68.5 km, hypsometric integral 0.23. 428 km of streams at 1 km², highest Strahler order 5, drainage density 0.83 km/km² (1.07 at 0.5 km² and 0.45 at 5 km²). OpenStreetMap maps 765.9 km of waterways inside the catchment; 53% of that length lies within 90 m of a derived stream, and 85% of the derived length lies within 90 m of a mapped waterway.

Limits

Demonstration on open data, not a client project. Terrain analysis for a hydrology study, not a hydrology or flood study: no rainfall, runoff, discharge or flood level is computed. NASADEM records the surface of February 2000 with about 30 m cells; SRTM-based heights carry absolute errors of about 5 to 9 m (90% level, Rodríguez et al. 2006), more on steep slopes, and include tree canopy and buildings, so streams are least reliable in forest, in towns and on flat ground. Reservoirs, canals, culverts and changes since 2000 are not represented. The stream threshold sets how dense the network looks. Madar provides spatial support only; any hydrology study is signed by a licensed office.
