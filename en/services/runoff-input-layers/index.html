Runoff Input Layers: Land Cover, Soils and Curve Number

Land-cover, hydrologic soil group, curve-number and impervious-surface layers prepared for the study engineer from the engineer's value table.

Quoted per scope

Delivered with a licensed office or specialist who signs the output

Madar provides spatial support only; signing belongs to a licensed office.

Request this service

The problem

A hydrological model needs current, consistent input layers for every basin, and preparing them takes more of the engineer's time than the modelling.

Who it is for

Offices qualified for hydrology studies, and stormwater engineers.

What you receive

  • A current land-cover map with an accuracy assessment.
  • A hydrologic soil group map from available sources.
  • A curve-number grid and the mean per sub-basin.
  • Impervious share per basin.
  • A surface-geology map from Saudi Geological Survey maps.
  • A sources and accuracy note.

What you supply

  • Basin boundaries or outlets.
  • The engineer's curve-number table.
  • Soil or borehole data, if any.

How the work goes

  1. The office fills in the request form, with the site and engineer's name.
  2. We agree the sources, classes and table.
  3. We classify land cover and check its accuracy.
  4. We prepare soils and geology and compute the curve number per basin.
  5. We deliver the layers, tables and note. The acceptance period is 7 days.

Accuracy and limits statement

Land cover is at 10 m; soil groups come from global sources at 250 m unless borehole data exists. The curve number is a spatial application of the engineer's table, not a calibration. The layers are inputs to a study, not a study.

Regulated work

A hydrology study is prepared and signed by a qualified office. The curve-number table and antecedent moisture conditions are set by the engineer; Madar applies them spatially.

Out of scope

  • Choosing curve-number values
  • Hydrological modelling
  • Boreholes and soil testing
  • Signing
